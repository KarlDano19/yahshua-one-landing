Every Filipino employer is legally required to deduct and remit three statutory contributions for each employee every payroll period: **SSS**, **PhilHealth**, and **Pag-IBIG**. Computing them incorrectly — or failing to remit on time — exposes your business to government penalties that compound fast.

This guide covers the current contribution tables, what each employer must pay, and the remittance deadlines you cannot miss.

---

## SSS (Social Security System)

SSS contributions fund retirement, disability, sickness, maternity, and death benefits for private-sector employees.

### Contribution Rate

As of 2025, the combined SSS contribution rate is **15% of Monthly Salary Credit (MSC)**:

- **Employee share:** 5% of MSC
- **Employer share:** 10% of MSC

The MSC is based on the employee's monthly compensation, capped at a **maximum of ₱35,000** and a **minimum of ₱5,000**.

### How to Compute

1. Determine the employee's monthly basic pay (plus regular allowances).
2. Look up the corresponding MSC in the SSS contribution table.
3. Compute deductions: employee deduction = 5% × MSC; employer share = 10% × MSC.

**Example:** An employee earns ₱20,000/month.
- MSC: ₱20,000
- Employee deduction: ₱1,000
- Employer contribution: ₱2,000
- Total remitted by employer: ₱3,000

### Remittance Deadline

SSS contributions must be remitted on the **last day of the month following the applicable period**. Contributions for January payroll are due by February 28.

## PhilHealth (Philippine Health Insurance Corporation)

PhilHealth contributions fund inpatient and outpatient health benefits for employees and their dependents.

### Contribution Rate

In 2025, the PhilHealth premium rate is **5% of Monthly Basic Salary (MBS)**:

- **Employee share:** 2.5% of MBS
- **Employer share:** 2.5% of MBS

The MBS is subject to:
- **Minimum floor:** ₱10,000 (minimum monthly contribution of ₱500 total — ₱250 each)
- **Maximum ceiling:** ₱100,000 (maximum monthly contribution of ₱5,000 total — ₱2,500 each)

### How to Compute

1. Get the employee's monthly basic salary.
2. Multiply by 5% for the total premium.
3. Split evenly: 2.5% each for employee and employer.

**Example:** An employee earns ₱30,000/month.
- Total premium: ₱30,000 × 5% = ₱1,500
- Employee deduction: ₱750
- Employer share: ₱750

### Remittance Deadline

Remittance is due on the **last day of the month following the applicable period** — same schedule as SSS.

## Pag-IBIG (Home Development Mutual Fund)

Pag-IBIG contributions fund housing loans, short-term multi-purpose loans, and forced savings for employees.

### Contribution Rate

The monthly contribution is based on the employee's monthly compensation:

| Monthly Compensation | Employee Share | Employer Share |
|---|---|---|
| ₱1,500 and below | 1% | 2% |
| Above ₱1,500 | 2% | 2% |

The **maximum monthly compensation used for computation is ₱5,000**, so the maximum contribution per employee is ₱100 (employee) and ₱100 (employer) — **₱200 total per month**.

### How to Compute

1. Determine the employee's monthly compensation.
2. Apply the rate (1% or 2% for employee; always 2% for employer).
3. Cap the computation base at ₱5,000.

**Example:** An employee earns ₱25,000/month.
- Computation base: ₱5,000 (capped)
- Employee deduction: ₱5,000 × 2% = ₱100
- Employer contribution: ₱5,000 × 2% = ₱100
- Total remitted: ₱200

### Remittance Deadline

Pag-IBIG contributions must be remitted by the **10th or 15th day of the month following the applicable period**, depending on your employer classification.

## What Employers Are Legally Required to Do

Under the SSS Law (RA 11199), National Health Insurance Act, and HDMF Charter, every employer must:

1. **Deduct** the employee share from each payroll period.
2. **Add** the employer share on top — this is a cost the employer bears, not an additional deduction from the employee.
3. **Remit** the full amount to each agency by the deadline.
4. **Report** new employees within 30 days of hiring.
5. **Keep records** of all contributions for at least 10 years.

**Penalties for late or non-remittance:**
- **SSS:** 3% penalty per month of delay
- **PhilHealth:** 2% penalty per month of delay, plus possible criminal liability for officers
- **Pag-IBIG:** 1/10 of 1% penalty per day of delay

## Common Mistakes Filipino Employers Make

**Using gross pay instead of basic pay for PhilHealth.** PhilHealth contributions are based on *monthly basic salary*, not total compensation. Allowances, overtime pay, and bonuses are generally excluded from the computation base.

**Forgetting to register new employees immediately.** New hires must be registered with SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG within 30 days of their start date. Late registration creates coverage gaps and penalty exposure.

**Not updating contribution tables.** SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG rates have changed multiple times in recent years under RA 11199 and the Universal Health Care Act. Always use the current year's official schedule.

**Treating the 13th month pay as subject to deductions.** The 13th month pay itself is exempt from SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG deductions. However, it counts toward the annual tax computation under BIR rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are contributions required for part-time employees?**

Yes. Any employee under an employer-employee relationship — full-time, part-time, or project-based — is covered. Contributions are based on their actual monthly compensation.

**Can employer and employee agree to waive statutory contributions?**

No. These are statutory obligations. Any agreement to skip or reduce contributions is illegal and void. Employers remain liable regardless of employee consent or written waiver.

**What if an employee is already registered as self-employed with SSS or Pag-IBIG?**

The employer is still required to enroll and remit for them as an employee. Their self-employed membership may be maintained separately, but it does not replace the employer obligation.

**What happens if I overpay contributions?**

SSS and PhilHealth both have correction procedures. Overpayments can generally be applied to future months. Underpayments require additional remittances and may carry penalties. YAHSHUA One flags contribution discrepancies automatically during each payroll run.
