An AI payroll buyer's checklist needs eight questions, each scored 0 to 2 on your own data: does the AI answer from your records, what can it change, what can it never do, whose permissions does it use, can you switch it off, what record does it leave, who is accountable, and what does it cost.

Vendors describe AI in similar words, so a brochure tells you little. Score the demo instead: 0 if they cannot show it, 1 if they say yes but cannot show it on your data, 2 if they show it on your own data. The total tells you whether to pilot.

## Why an AI Payroll Checklist Needs Scores, Not Claims

Public AI claims in payroll are hard to compare, because the same words cover very different products. Two public pages show it: one vendor says its AI "never runs payroll or approves changes", while another says its agent "does the task for you, and asks before anything important happens".

Neither line says what it takes to see that behavior on your data. One of those pages also labels a payroll AI block "coming soon" beside a present-tense description. The point is not that either vendor is wrong. A claim is a starting point, and a score is a result.

You also have a legal stake. Under NPC Advisory No. 2024-04, the personal information controller stays accountable for AI processing, including when the work is subcontracted (Sec. 2(B)), and must be able to explain the purpose of the processing and the factors the system considers (Sec. 2(A)). Whether a given task meets the advisory's "significant risk" test is a question for your counsel. A vendor who cannot answer in writing makes your own explanation harder.

## Questions 1 to 3: What the AI Can Read and Change

Start with the AI's reach: whether it answers from your records, what it can change and what it needs before it does, and what it can never do. A vendor that cannot list its limits has not defined them. Score each question in the demo, on your own setup, not on slides.

**1. Does it answer from your own records?** Ask something only your data can answer, such as one employee's rate type or your last five payroll runs. **Score 2** if the answer is right and comes from your records. **Score 0** if you get a textbook answer or a pointer to a report.

**2. What can it change, and what has to happen first?** Ask for the full list of actions the AI can take, then watch one. Look for a named role and an explicit confirmation before anything changes. **Score 2** if the list is written, one action is shown live, and the confirmation step is visible.

**3. What can it never do?** Ask whether it can approve a payroll run, post it or delete records. **Score 2** if the vendor gives a written list of actions the AI cannot take and shows one being refused. **Score 0** if the answer is "it depends".

## Questions 4 to 7: Who Stays in Control of AI Payroll

Control comes from permissions, an off switch, a record and a named person. Test each one as a limited user, not as an administrator, because an administrator login hides the failures that matter. The same questions apply whether the AI is a chat window, an agent or a connector to another tool.

**4. Does it use each user's own permissions?** Log in as a user without salary access and ask for salary details. **Score 2** if the AI refuses or gives a reduced answer. **Score 0** if it answers anyway.

**5. Can you switch it off?** Ask whether the AI can be turned off for the whole company, or limited to named roles, and who is allowed to change that setting. **Score 2** if you see the setting and can show who controls it.

**6. What record does it leave?** Ask to see what the AI did last week and who said yes: the request, the action, the person and the time. **Score 2** if you can pull that record yourself. **Score 0** if the vendor has to "check with engineering".

**7. Who is accountable for the final run?** Ask who approves each payroll run and whether the AI can approve its own work. **Score 2** if a named person approves every run and the AI cannot. NPC Advisory 2024-04 expects meaningful human intervention, by people with the necessary competence and authority, where automated decisions pose a significant risk (Sec. 2(B)(2)(a)). Whether payroll meets that threshold is for your counsel, but a named approver is the control that makes the question easy to answer.

## Question 8: What the AI Costs

Ask how AI usage is priced and whether you can see the meter. Many AI features bill by usage, so a vendor that cannot show a balance or an invoice line cannot tell you what a busy payroll week will cost. Score 2 only if the pricing is written down and the usage is visible in the product.

**8. How is AI usage priced, and can you see the meter?** Ask for the price basis (per user, per credit or flat) and where usage and balance appear. **Score 2** if both are shown. **Score 0** if the answer is "it is included for now".

## Score the Vendor: The Eight Questions in One Table

Add the eight scores for a total out of 16. Our thresholds, which are not an official standard: 13 to 16 is ready to pilot, 8 to 12 is a pilot only if each gap is fixed in writing first, and 7 or less is not ready. Print the table and fill it in during the demo.

**Table: The eight questions, what to ask to see, and what scores 2**

| # | Question | Ask to see | Scores 2 if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Answers from your records? | One question only your data can answer | The answer is right and comes from your records |
| 2 | What can it change? | The written list of actions, and one live | A named role and an explicit confirmation are visible |
| 3 | What can it never do? | The written list of refused actions | One refusal is shown live |
| 4 | Uses each user's permissions? | A limited user asking for salary details | The AI refuses or reduces the answer |
| 5 | Can you switch it off? | The setting, and who can change it | It can be turned off or limited to named roles |
| 6 | What record does it leave? | The last week of AI actions | You can pull request, action, person and time yourself |
| 7 | Who is accountable? | Who approves each run | A named person approves, and the AI cannot approve its own work |
| 8 | What does it cost? | The price basis and the usage meter | The pricing is written and usage is visible in the product |

## Three Questions to Put in Writing Before You Sign

Do not score these in the demo. Ask for written answers: where your data is processed and by which providers, whether it is used to train models, and how long AI conversations and tool results are kept. As the employer, you remain accountable for what a vendor does with payroll data (NPC Advisory 2024-04, Sec. 2(B)).

These are the answers your privacy officer needs to explain the system to employees (Sec. 2(A)) and to handle requests to object, correct or delete (Sec. 3). Ask whether training is off by default or only on request, and whether conversations can be deleted. A vague answer is a signal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I ask an AI payroll vendor in the Philippines?**
Eight things, on your own data: whether the AI answers from your records, what it can change, what it can never do, whether it uses each user's permissions, whether you can switch it off, what record it leaves, who is accountable for the final run, and how usage is priced. Also ask for written answers on where your data is processed, whether it is used to train models, and how long conversations are kept.

**What is the difference between an AI that answers and an AI that acts?**
An AI that answers reads your records and explains them. An AI that acts changes a record, starts a workflow or files something. Ask which actions exist, who must confirm each one, and what the AI can never do. Our sibling site YAHSHUA HRIS covers the same split from the compliance side.

**Does the Data Privacy Act apply to AI in payroll?**
Yes, where personal data is processed. NPC Advisory No. 2024-04 (December 19, 2024) applies the Data Privacy Act to AI systems that process personal data. Payroll data is personal data, and the employer stays accountable for the processing even when a vendor carries it out (Sec. 2(B)).

**How do I score an AI payroll demo?**
Give each of the eight questions 0 if the vendor cannot show it, 1 if the vendor says yes but cannot show it on your data, and 2 if it is shown on your own data. A total of 13 to 16 is ready to pilot, 8 to 12 means pilot only with each gap fixed in writing, and 7 or less is not ready. These thresholds are ours, not a standard.

**Should a person approve every payroll run even when AI helps?**
As a control, yes: keep a named person approving every run, and do not let the AI approve its own work. NPC Advisory 2024-04 expects meaningful human intervention by people with the necessary competence and authority where automated decisions pose a significant risk (Sec. 2(B)(2)(a)). Whether a given payroll task meets that threshold is a question for your counsel.
