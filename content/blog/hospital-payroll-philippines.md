Hospital payroll in the Philippines needs seven controls: confirm which rule covers each facility, pay the sixth-day premium, split night hours correctly, use one premium table, pay shifts actually worked, hold agency-hired staff to the same standard, and separate who schedules, approves and pays. Each can be tested in ten minutes.

A hospital runs every pay rule at once: shifts that cross midnight, work on holidays, a sixth day when a ward is short, and nurses supplied by an agency. The hard part is rarely the arithmetic. It is the rosters, the approvals and a statute that applies only to health personnel.

## What Makes Hospital Payroll in the Philippines Different

Hospitals run on a 24-hour roster, so one pay period can trigger overtime, night differential, rest day and holiday rules together. A separate Labor Code rule (Art. 83) applies only to health personnel, and agency-supplied staff add shared liability. This guide covers private hospitals; government health workers follow RA 7305.

**Table: The rules behind the seven controls**

| Rule | Source | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Hours for health personnel | Labor Code Art. 83 | In hospitals and clinics with at least 100 beds, and in cities or municipalities of at least 1,000,000 people, health personnel hold regular hours of 8 a day, 5 days a week. Six days or 48 hours is allowed when the service requires it, with additional compensation of at least 30% of the regular wage for the sixth day. |
| Night shift differential | Art. 86; DOLE-BWC Handbook 2024 | An additional 10% of the regular wage for each hour worked from 10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. |
| Overtime and holiday pay | Arts. 87 and 91 to 94; DOLE-BWC Handbook 2024 | Overtime on an ordinary day is 125%, a regular holiday worked is 200%, and the handbook table gives every stacked case. |
| Agency-supplied staff | DOLE DO 174-17, Secs. 5 and 9 | Labor-only contracting is prohibited, and the principal and the contractor are solidarily liable for Labor Code violations, including failure to pay wages. |

We do not repeat statutory rates beyond what each control needs. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Control 1: Confirm Which Health-Personnel Rule Covers Each Facility

Test every facility against two conditions in Labor Code Art. 83: a bed capacity of at least 100, or a location in a city or municipality of at least 1,000,000 people. If either holds, health personnel keep regular hours of 8 a day, 5 days a week. The term covers nurses, attendants and all other hospital or clinic personnel.

A group with several sites can get a different answer for each one. Write down the basis for every facility, with its bed capacity and city, so the rule is applied on purpose and not by habit. Government hospitals are outside this rule.

**Test:** list each facility with its bed capacity and city. Ask payroll which Art. 83 test each one meets, and who decided.

## Control 2: Pay the Sixth-Day Premium When the Roster Requires It

When the service requires six days or 48 hours, Art. 83 gives covered health personnel additional compensation of at least 30% of their regular wage for work on the sixth day. The roster triggers it, not the employee, so payroll must detect a sixth worked day in the week and pay it.

The article is narrow. In San Juan de Dios Hospital Employees Association v. NLRC (G.R. No. 126383, November 28, 1997), the Supreme Court held that Art. 83 provides the 8-hour, 5-day schedule and the sixth-day premium, and does not support a claim to a full weekly wage with two paid days off. A company policy or a collective bargaining agreement can give more.

Decide in writing how the sixth-day premium relates to your rest-day premium, ask your counsel to confirm it, and set the payroll rule to match. The answer should not depend on one payroll officer's memory.

**Test:** pick a nurse who worked six days last week. Ask to see the roster that required it and the payroll line that paid the sixth-day premium.

## Control 3: Split Night Shift Differential Hours Across Midnight

Night shift differential is an additional 10% of the regular wage for each hour worked from 10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. (Labor Code Art. 86). A shift that starts or ends outside that window is part night and part day, so the hours must be split, not paid at one rate for the whole shift.

A 7:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m. block has 8 hours inside the window and 4 outside it. The differential belongs on the 8. Paying it on all 12 overpays, and paying it on none underpays.

The differential does not apply to government employees, kasambahay, retail and service establishments regularly employing not more than five workers, managerial employees who meet all three DOLE tests, or field and unsupervised personnel. Nurses and other clinical staff do not appear on that list. Check any ward head you call managerial against all three tests before you exclude them.

**Test:** take one 7:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m. shift from the last cutoff. Ask payroll how many hours received the 10% and why. The answer should be 8.

## Control 4: Let One Premium Table Decide Overtime, Rest Day and Holiday Pay

Adopt one written table of multipliers from the DOLE-BWC Handbook on Workers' Statutory Monetary Benefits (2024) and make payroll reproduce its hardest cases. A regular holiday that falls on a rest day, worked at night, is 286%. With overtime on top it is 371.8%.

Hospitals meet these cases often, because wards run through holidays and rest days. How the rates stack is covered in our sibling site's guide, [Night Differential and Holiday Pay: How the Rates Actually Stack](https://yahshuahris.com/blog/night-differential-holiday-pay-philippines), so we do not repeat its tables. The control is about ownership: someone must own the table, the holiday calendar, and the check that payroll still matches both.

The calendar changes during the year. Movable regular holidays are fixed by a presidential proclamation each year, and other special days can be declared (DOLE-BWC Handbook).

**Test:** ask payroll to compute one regular holiday that falls on a rest day, worked on the night shift, with overtime. Compare the multipliers with 286% and 371.8%.

## Control 5: Pay the Shift Actually Worked, Not the Posted Roster

Pay from supervisor-approved attendance, not from the posted roster. Rotating schedules, swaps and cover shifts change who worked what after the roster is published, and premium pay depends on the actual hours and the kind of day worked.

Keep each swap as a record with both employees, the hours each one worked and the person who approved it, and let payroll read the approved record. A roster edited after the cutoff closes can change pay without anyone noticing. For attendance controls that apply at any site, see our [multi-branch payroll controls](/blog/multi-branch-payroll-philippines).

**Test:** pick one swap from the last cutoff. Ask who approved it, and check that each employee's payslip matches the hours that employee actually worked.

## Control 6: Hold Agency-Hired Staff to the Same Pay Standard

If an agency supplies your nurses or other staff under a contracting arrangement, DOLE Department Order 174-17 makes you and the agency solidarily liable for Labor Code violations, including failure to pay wages, to the extent of the work performed (Sec. 9). Ask for the agency's DOLE registration and proof that its people receive the statutory premiums.

The order prohibits labor-only contracting (Sec. 5). It requires contractors to register with DOLE and presumes labor-only contracting when they do not. Permissible contracting needs, among other conditions, substantial capital, defined as at least ₱5,000,000 in paid-up capital for a corporation, and a service agreement that secures the contractor's employees' labor-law rights (Sec. 8). If an agency is found to be a labor-only contractor, you are treated as the direct employer (Sec. 7).

A DOLE review of the order was under way in November 2024 (ECCP position paper), and we found no amending order. Check DOLE's issuances before you rely on this.

**Test:** ask for the agency's DOLE registration and its last two payroll registers for staff placed with you. Check that night differential, overtime and holiday lines appear where the roster says they should.

## Control 7: Separate Who Schedules, Who Approves and Who Pays

Give scheduling to the ward manager, attendance and overtime approval to a supervisor, and the payroll run to payroll, with a different person approving it. Salary data is personal data, and the employer must use reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical and technical measures to protect it (RA 10173, Sec. 20).

A ward manager who can edit the roster, approve the overtime and see everyone's pay can change an outcome without anyone noticing. Keep a change history so you can show who changed a roster, a rate or an approval, and when. Whether your setup meets the legal standard is a question for your counsel.

**Test:** ask who approved overtime on the busiest ward last cutoff, who prepared the run and who approved it. They should be three different people. Then log in as a ward manager and try to open another ward's payslips.

Print this table and ask your payroll lead to show each answer on your own data, not in a demo account. A control passes only if the answer comes from the system, not from someone's memory.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do nurses get night differential in the Philippines?**
Yes, if they are covered. Night shift differential is an additional 10% of the regular wage for each hour worked from 10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. (Labor Code Art. 86). It does not apply to government employees, kasambahay, retail and service establishments regularly employing not more than five workers, qualifying managerial employees or field personnel. Nurses and other clinical staff of private hospitals are not on that list.

**What is the sixth-day premium for hospital staff?**
Where the service requires six days or 48 hours, health personnel in hospitals and clinics with at least 100 beds, or in cities and municipalities of at least 1,000,000 people, are entitled to additional compensation of at least 30% of their regular wage for work on the sixth day (Labor Code Art. 83).

**Are hospital employees entitled to two paid days off after a 40-hour week?**
Not under Art. 83 alone. In San Juan de Dios Hospital Employees Association v. NLRC (G.R. No. 126383, November 28, 1997), the Supreme Court held that Art. 83 provides the 8-hour, 5-day schedule and the sixth-day premium, and does not support a full weekly wage with two paid days off. A company policy or a collective bargaining agreement can still provide more.

**Who is liable if an agency underpays nurses placed at our hospital?**
Under DOLE Department Order 174-17, Sec. 9, the principal and the contractor are solidarily liable for violations of the Labor Code, including failure to pay wages, to the extent of the work performed under the employment contract. If the agency is a labor-only contractor, the principal is treated as the direct employer (Sec. 7). Check the agency's DOLE registration.

**Do government hospital workers follow the same pay rules?**
No. The sixth-day rule in Art. 83 does not apply to health workers in government service. Their benefits are defined in RA 7305, the Magna Carta of Public Health Workers. This guide covers private hospitals.
